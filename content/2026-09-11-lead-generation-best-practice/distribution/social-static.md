# Social static — 3 assets

Derived from `source.md`. Designed as organic LinkedIn image posts (the pack's approved organic-social channel for this brand) rather than for a general consumer-social platform. Art direction avoids any depiction of injured people, actors standing in for clients, or anything that could read as a testimonial or case result — none of that is approved for this brand, and static image assets tend to invite that shortcut more than plain text does.

---

## Asset 1 — "Vet. Test. Deliver. Manage."

**Copy on image (headline):** Vet. Test. Deliver. Manage.
**Copy on image (subhead):** Vetting a lead source is stage one. Most vendor relationships stop there. Ours doesn't.

**Caption (below image):**
A vetting checklist tells you what a provider says about itself. It can't tell you whether the source performs, because it hasn't happened yet. That's why we treat vetting as the first of four stages, not the whole process. Full piece: [ARTICLE]

**Art direction:** Four-step horizontal or vertical diagram, one word per step (Vet / Test / Deliver / Manage), simple arrows or dividers between them, no icons that imply speed or ease (no rockets, checkmarks-as-guarantee, etc.). Clean, editorial layout: brand type on a solid or subtly textured background, no stock photography of people. Understated, not a hard-sell graphic.

## Asset 2 — "An answer is not evidence"

**Copy on image (headline):** A vendor comfortable being vetted will give you a comfortable answer.

**Copy on image (subhead, smaller):** That's not the same as a source that performs.

**Caption (below image):**
Every vetting checklist asks the right questions. None of them can predict what happens once a source is actually running against your intake team. Here's the difference between a good answer and real evidence: [ARTICLE]

**Art direction:** Quote-card treatment. Large-type headline as the visual focus, generous white space, no imagery beyond brand color/texture. Should read as a considered, quotable statement, not an ad.

## Asset 3 — "What a questionnaire can't see yet"

**Copy on image (headline):** What does volume and quality look like 90 days in, not week one?

**Copy on image (subhead, smaller):** Your vendor questionnaire didn't ask. Neither did theirs.

**Caption (below image):**
No vendor can answer that one honestly before you've actually worked together. Only time, and someone watching, can. Three more questions like it, and what we actually do about them: [ARTICLE]

**Art direction:** Same quote-card system as Asset 2, for visual consistency across the set (same type scale, same background treatment) so the three read as one series rather than three unrelated posts.
