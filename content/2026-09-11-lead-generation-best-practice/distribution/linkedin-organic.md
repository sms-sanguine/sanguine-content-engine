## Post 1 — "The checklist isn't wrong"

Every vetting checklist for lead vendors asks the right questions. Is this vendor generating its own leads, or reselling something bought elsewhere and marked up? What's the consent process? Is the lead exclusive?

Good questions. Ask them.

Here's what they can't do: predict whether the answer holds up once that source is actually running against your intake team, your case types, your market.

A vendor comfortable being vetted gives a comfortable answer. That's not the same as a source that performs.

At Sanguine Legal Solutions, vetting is stage one of four: vet, test, deliver, manage. A source gets tested against real performance before it's introduced more broadly, and we stay involved after a firm signs, because that's where most of these relationships are actually won or lost.

## Post 2 — "A vendor comfortable being vetted"

A vendor comfortable being vetted will give you a comfortable answer.

That's not an accusation. It's just what a questionnaire is: a chance for a provider to describe itself, before a single lead has reached your intake team.

It can't tell you whether the case types match what you actually want. Whether the volume holds at your geography. Whether delivery is still what it was 90 days in, not week one.

That's the gap between "answered the questionnaire well" and "will actually perform for this firm." It's also where a lot of lead-generation budget quietly disappears.

Our model treats vetting as the first of four stages, not the whole process: vet, test, deliver, manage. We wrote up why that distinction matters, and what we actually watch for after a source goes live: [ARTICLE]

## Post 3 — "Ask what happens after signing"

Most vendor checklists stop at the contract. Here's what they never ask:

→ What does volume and quality look like 90 days in, not week one?
→ Who's accountable when delivery slows down, and what's the actual process for raising it?
→ Is anyone still checking that the source performs the way it did in testing, six months in?

No vendor can answer these honestly before you've worked together. Only time, and someone actually watching, can.
